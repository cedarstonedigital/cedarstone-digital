# Finding work that does not ask for a portfolio

Prepared 26 September 2026. Target markets: UK and US.

## The actual problem

CedarStone has two paid clients with live sites, lrtthreadz.com and
sesothofashioneng.co.za. Most formal briefs want three examples plus three
referees. That gap is why the ERC bid stalled, and it will stall the next one
too.

So the question is not "where is web work" but "who hires without asking to see
a client list". There are three honest answers.

---

## 1. White label work for other agencies. Best fit by a distance.

An agency already has the portfolio, the client relationship and the sales
process. They are not buying your reputation, they are buying build capacity.
Nobody asks a subcontractor for references from their own clients.

It also pays on delivery, repeats monthly, and you keep no account management.

**Where these appear, check daily.** r/wordpressjobs, r/forhire (filter
`[Hiring]`, ignore `[For Hire]` which is other freelancers), r/developers_hire,
r/hiredev, r/freelance_forhire, r/jobbit.

Search the phrase "white label" alongside "web" on those subs. Most white label
posts never reach page one of Google.

**What to send them.** Not a portfolio. Send: the stack you build in, your
turnaround on a five page site, your rate, and whether you can work to their
mockups. Agencies care about reliability and speed, not taste.

---

## 2. Businesses with no website at all

When the alternative is nothing, nobody asks for references. They ask what it
costs and how fast.

**The categories that reliably have none.** Checked barbers in Leeds and
Manchester as a sample. Almost every one had a Booksy or Fresha booking link
and no site of their own. That is the pattern to hunt:

- Barbers, nail bars, hair salons, tattoo studios, beauty rooms
- Independent takeaways and cafes
- Mobile trades: mechanics, valeters, dog groomers, mobile hairdressers
- Small builders, plasterers, painters, landscapers, man and van
- Personal trainers and small gyms

These run on Instagram, Facebook or a booking link. They are findable,
reachable by phone, and the decision maker is the person who answers.

**How to build the list.** Google Maps, search "<category> <town>", then open
each listing. The ones with no Website button are the list. Record name, phone,
and what they use instead (Instagram, Booksy, Facebook page). Roughly one in
three in the categories above will have nothing.

Work small UK towns rather than London or Manchester centre. Less competition,
same budgets, and a local voice carries further. Same in the US: mid size
towns, not New York or LA.

**The opener that works on this group.** Build the thing first. One page, their
real photos pulled from their Instagram, their real opening hours. Send the
link, not a pitch. "I built this for you, it is live at this address, it is
yours for X if you want it." Nobody asks for a portfolio when they are looking
at their own business on a screen.

That converts because it removes every objection at once: no brief, no meeting,
no risk, and visible proof you can actually do it.

---

## 3. Where people ask out loud

Public Facebook group posts asking for web help exist and are findable. Active
examples came up in these groups this week, including several where a business
owner is asking directly rather than a freelancer advertising.

**Groups to join and watch.** Search Facebook for: "small business owners UK",
"UK small business network", "small business support group", plus your target
town's name with "business". US equivalents: "small business owners USA",
"<state> small business".

**Honest limitation.** I can see public group posts through search, but I cannot
monitor groups properly without being logged in, and driving your Chrome has
been unreliable this session. This one needs you inside the groups, checking
daily. It is fifteen minutes a day and it is free.

**Other places people ask in the open:** r/smallbusiness, r/Entrepreneur,
r/AskUK, Nextdoor for US local, and local town Facebook pages.

## What I would actually do this week

1. **Reply to the r/wordpressjobs recurring local business post today.** It is
   three days old with 73 comments, so move fast. Recurring agency work is the
   single fastest route out of the portfolio problem.
2. **Build three speculative one pagers** for real businesses with no site.
   Barbers or takeaways in one UK town. Send them cold with the link live.
   Three sent, expect one reply. Even unpaid, a live site you built for a real
   named business is portfolio.
3. **Join five Facebook groups** and check them once a day.

Two of those cost nothing but time, and all three produce portfolio whether or
not the money lands.

## What to stop doing for now

Formal RFPs. They ask for three clients and three referees, they take days to
answer, and the odds are poor against agencies with fifteen case studies. Come
back to them when the portfolio is six or eight real sites deep.

The ERC work is parked, not deleted. It sits in the repo and remains one of the
strongest things CedarStone has built, which makes it useful as portfolio even
though the bid is not being submitted.
