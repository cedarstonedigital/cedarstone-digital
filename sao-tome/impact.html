São Tomé & Príncipe
Sustainability · Chapter 03

Tourism & the SDGs

How visitors can create decent local jobs, protect turtles and rainforest, and how technology, used carefully, can help.

Fishing beach, São Tomé · Wikimedia Commons ↗
The challenge

Why tourism matters here

Since the 19th century the islands' economy has depended on plantation agriculture, especially cocoa. The country imports most of its food; in 1997 about 90% of its food needs were met by imports. The biggest economic challenges it faces are a small workforce, a tiny domestic market, the cost of being an archipelago, climatic fluctuations, global warming and poverty.

Tourism is widely seen as the sector with the greatest potential to diversify the economy. Foreign investment is growing and new lodges are opening. The question this project asks is how that growth can reach families in Neves, Angolares and Santo António, and protect the forests and beaches that visitors come for.

UN Sustainable Development Goals

The goals this project supports

8SDG
Primary goal

Decent Work & Economic Growth

Target 8.9 calls for policies that promote sustainable tourism which creates jobs and promotes local culture and products. In São Tomé that means guides, drivers, cooks, craftspeople and roça staff earning directly from visitors, instead of the money leaving the islands through imports and foreign-owned packages.

  • Visitors: hire certified local guides, and buy island chocolate, coffee and crafts.
  • Businesses: train and promote local staff, and buy from local farmers and fishers.
14SDG

Life Below Water

The islands' beaches are nesting grounds for marine turtles, including the critically endangered hawksbill, and the surrounding sea supports the fishing communities. Paid turtle-watching gives a nesting female more value alive than the income she once brought to a hunter.

  • Visitors: join guided night walks only, use red light, and never buy turtle-shell products.
  • Operators: fund beach patrols and hire former hunters as guardians.
15SDG

Life on Land

Obô Natural Park protects endemic species such as the giant sunbird and the São Tomé ibis. Park fees and guided hikes give local people a steady income from keeping the forest standing.

  • Visitors: stay on the trails, pay park fees and carry your rubbish out.
  • Planners: set visitor limits for fragile sites such as Lagoa Amélia.
12SDG

Responsible Consumption & Production

Target 12.b is about developing tools to monitor the effects of sustainable tourism. For a small island state, simple digital monitoring of visitor numbers, spending and waste turns good intentions into evidence.

  • Visitors: bring a refillable bottle and avoid single-use plastic.
  • Government: publish open data on visitor numbers and where their money goes.
Community & environment

How tourism can help

Bringing the roças back to life. Restoring plantation buildings as guesthouses, museums and restaurants keeps heritage alive and creates year-round jobs in villages that grew up around the estates.

Adding value to cocoa. A bag of beans exported in bulk earns little. A tasting, a chocolate workshop or a bar made on the island earns many times more, and it tells visitors the story of the Chocolate Islands.

Buying local food. When hotels buy from local farmers and fishers, the country imports less, farm incomes rise and guests eat better.

Paying for conservation. Park fees, guide wages and turtle-watching income make conservation pay. Forest and beaches then earn more protected than cleared.

Cultural pride. Audiences for Tchiloli, socopé and creole literature give young São Toméans a reason to learn these traditions and a way to earn from them.

Tech-enabled solutions

Practical ideas for sustainable growth

  1. AI trip personalisationSDG 8 · visibility

    Itinerary tools, like the planner on this site, that deliberately route visitors to community stays, guides and restaurants and spread them across districts and seasons, not just the few best-known beaches.

  2. Multilingual AI conciergeInclusivity

    A chatbot on WhatsApp that answers in Portuguese, English and French, runs on low bandwidth, and hands bookings to local operators who have no website of their own.

  3. Community booking & mobile paymentsSDG 8 · fair income

    One shared, low-fee listing for guides, homestays and drivers, paid by mobile money, so payments reach families and are not taken by foreign platforms.

  4. AI-assisted wildlife monitoringSDG 14 & 15

    Camera traps and drone surveys analysed by AI to count turtle nests and detect forest loss, giving rangers data they can act on and giving visitors evidence of the impact of their fees.

  5. QR heritage trailsCulture

    QR codes at roças, forts and churches linking to short audio stories told by local voices, in Forro as well as Portuguese and English, so heritage can earn income without adding new buildings.

  6. Visitor-impact dashboardSDG 12.b

    Open data on arrivals, local spending, waste and pressure on each site, used to set limits for fragile places such as Lagoa Amélia before damage is done.

Critical assessment

What could go wrong

Internet is good in towns but patchy in the interior, and many small operators have limited digital skills. Global booking platforms can take a large share of the money, and data about communities can be collected without consent.

How to get it right

Design principles

Build offline-first, low-bandwidth tools. Train local operators and give them ownership of their listings and data. Keep fees low, and measure success by the income that stays on the islands, not by clicks.
