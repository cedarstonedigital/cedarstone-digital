Program 01

There are enough things to weigh up when finding a home.

Where you live affects your access to work, schools, transport, healthcare, food, and your own safety. Housing discrimination narrows those options, and it keeps old patterns of segregation in place.

What it looks like in practice

It is usually quiet, polite, and hard to prove on your own.

Almost nobody says no outright. It arrives as a unit that was just taken, a deposit that went up after the viewing, a call that never came back. Any one of these can be nothing. Side by side with a matched tester, a pattern either shows or it does not.

Steering

Shown different neighbourhoods

Two applicants with the same budget are guided toward different parts of the city.

Terms

Quoted different deposits or fees

The advertised price holds for one applicant and moves for the other, or extra conditions appear.

Access

Availability that changes

A unit is available on one call and gone on the next, then available again.

Accommodation

Requests that go nowhere

A reasonable accommodation or modification request is refused, delayed indefinitely, or met with conditions that are not lawful.

Who is protected

Federal law sets the floor. This region goes further.

The federal Fair Housing Act applies everywhere. Maryland and Virginia each add categories on top of it, and individual counties and cities can add more again.

Federal Fair Housing Act

Applies in every state, including the District.

Race Colour Religion National origin Sex Disability Familial status, meaning children under 18

Added by state

Maryland adds marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity and source of income. Virginia adds elderliness at 55 and over, sexual orientation, gender identity, source of income and veteran status.

What we can do

You do not have to be sure before you call.

Most people who contact us are not certain what happened. Working that out is our job, not yours.

01

02

04

Making home accessible

Resources for renters, owners and housing providers.

A toolkit on reasonable accommodations and modifications, answers for housing providers, a verification template for third parties, and a plain explanation of service and assistance animals under the Fair Housing Act and the ADA.

You always have an independent right to file on your own. Complaints can go directly to the US Department of Housing and Urban Development, the DC Office of Human Rights, or the Fairfax County Office of Human Rights and Equity Programs. Talking to us does not replace that route and does not close it off.
