The Equal Rights Center advances equal access, opportunity, and fair treatment so individuals and communities can thrive. We use civil rights testing to document discrimination in housing, employment and public accommodations, then act on what the record shows.

The method

Discrimination is rarely announced. So we go and look.

Civil rights testing is how we turn a suspicion into a record. It is the core of everything below, and it is why our findings hold up in front of regulators, courts and the businesses themselves.

  1. 01

    Test

    Two testers approach the same landlord, employer or business. Their profiles are matched on everything that should matter. They differ on the one thing that should not.

  2. 02

    Document

    Each tester records the same details the same way. What was offered, what was quoted, which unit was shown, who got a call back, and what was said.

  3. 03

    Compare

    Side by side, the difference in treatment either exists or it does not. No one has to rely on memory or interpretation. The two accounts speak for themselves.

  4. 04

    Act

    Findings inform the public and the business community, support policy advocacy, and shape compliance training. Where it is needed, they support enforcement.

Programs

Five places where treatment gets unequal.

Housing, work, and the ordinary business of daily life. Each program runs on the same testing method and feeds the same body of evidence.

01

Fair Housing

Where you live shapes your access to work, schools, transport, healthcare, food and safety. We test rental and sales practices across DC, Maryland and Virginia, and help people who believe they were turned away unfairly.

Open 02

Disability and Accessibility

More than 65,000 retail locations, restaurants and homes have been made more accessible through this work. We also help residents request reasonable accommodations and modifications.

Open 03

Employment

Rules against employment discrimination protect people from unfair treatment on the job and support workplaces that function properly. We test hiring and staffing practices to see whether those rules hold in practice.

Open 04

Public Accommodations

Restaurants, shops, transport and services. For more than 30 years we have worked to extend civil rights protections to LGBTQ+ people and to people with limited English proficiency.

Open 05

Multifamily Housing Resource Program

Training and compliance support for housing providers who would rather get this right than be found out. Focusing on civil rights is not only the right thing to do, it is also smart business.

What the evidence changed

Agreements, rulings and settlements.

Testing on its own proves something. These are the points where proof turned into a change in how people are treated.

2026

Four DC properties agree to fair treatment for voucher holders

An agreement covering renters with housing vouchers, past evictions and criminal records at four properties in the District, reached with Vesta.

2025

A key ruling against Meta on housing advertising

Litigation over how housing adverts were targeted and delivered, described by observers as one of the most consequential technology cases in fair housing.

2024

Agreement with Uber on access for riders with disabilities

Testing of ride hailing practices led to an agreement on how riders with disabilities, including wheelchair users and people with service animals, are served.

2016

Equity Residential agrees to expand accessible housing

A long running case settled in January 2016, committing one of the country's largest apartment owners to accessibility work across its communities.

Know where you stand

The greater Washington region has some of the strongest housing protections in the country.

The federal Fair Housing Act sets the floor. Maryland and Virginia both go further. If a landlord or agent treated you differently for any reason below, that is worth telling us about.

Source of income Sexual orientation Gender identity Marital status Veteran status Elderliness, 55 and over

Marital status applies in Maryland. Veteran status and elderliness apply in Virginia. Protections vary by county and city.
