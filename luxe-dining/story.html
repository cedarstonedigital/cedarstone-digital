Luxe Dining Restaurant

Our Story

One room, one kitchen, and a simple promise: cook honestly, serve generously, and let the evening take as long as it takes.

Since 2009

It began with
a Sunday table

Luxe Dining started as a supper club of fourteen seats above a wine shop on Rivonia Road. The bookings outgrew the room within a year. Everything since — the move to Luxury Lane, the cellar, the private dining room — has been an attempt to keep that first evening's feeling at a larger scale.

Our philosophy

Fewer dishes,
done properly

Twenty-one dishes is a deliberately short menu. It means every sauce is made the morning it is served, every fish is filleted in-house, and the brigade can put its whole attention into a plate instead of spreading it across sixty.

We buy whole animals from two farms in the Free State and break them down here. The trim becomes the jus. The bones become the stock that becomes tomorrow's bisque. Nothing that can be used is thrown away — not out of fashion, but because that is how good food is made.

An evening at Luxe

The room, after dark

Candlelight, low conversation and a kitchen that does not stop until the last table has finished. This is what a Friday looks like from above.

What we stand on

Three things we
will not compromise

Sourcing

Beef from two Free State farms. Line-caught fish landed in Cape Town and driven up overnight. Vegetables from four Midlands growers we have used since 2011.

Craft

Stocks reduced over two days. Bread proved overnight. Ice cream churned each morning. No powders, no shortcuts, no plate leaving the pass that the chef would not eat himself.

Hospitality

One server to twelve guests. Water glasses filled before you ask. Allergies taken seriously and handled in the kitchen, not with an apology at the table.

The cellar

Forty by the glass,
four hundred by the bottle

Our list leans Cape — Hemel-en-Aarde pinot, Swartland syrah, Stellenbosch cabernet — with a deliberate Burgundy and Piedmont section for those who want it. Ask for Lerato; she will find you something better than what you were going to order, usually for less money.
